How to Set Daily App Limits on Google TV & Android TV

Updated: November 2026 • 4 min read • Parental Control Guide

Unlike smartphones, modern Smart TVs lack flexible built-in parental controls. While Google TV offers whole-device timers, it doesn't let parents set a 30-minute quota for video games or streaming apps while allowing other family members to use the television. Here is how to configure per-app screen time limits using TV Timer.

Why Per-App Limits Are Essential on Smart TVs

A single shared TV serves multiple family purposes: educational documentaries, Saturday cartoons, video games, and parent evening cinema. A blunt "lock the whole TV" timer causes friction. With TV Timer: Parental Control, you can:

  • Set 30 minutes for YouTube to prevent endless short-form video consumption.
  • Allow 1 hour for Netflix or Disney+ for family movie night.
  • Set strict 20-minute limits for installed Android TV games.
  • Keep the TV launcher, educational apps, or music apps freely accessible.

3 Core Features of TV Timer for Family Screen Habits

1. Independent Daily App Quotas

Assign individual timers to any installed TV app. Active foreground seconds count down in real-time. When the quota hits zero, the app is closed, and cannot be relaunched until the automatic midnight reset.

2. Continuous Viewing Breaks (Session Cooldowns)

Children's developing eyes need regular breaks. Even if a child has 90 minutes of daily TV allowance, TV Timer can force a mandatory 15-minute rest break after 45 minutes of continuous watching.

3. Adult Bypass Schedule (Free Hours)

Parents shouldn't have to unlock apps or consume children's daily allowances when watching TV during the day or late at night. Set up scheduled free hours (e.g. 10:00 AM – 3:00 PM on weekdays, or 9:00 PM – 12:00 AM) where screen time tracking automatically pauses.
